# Conclusion

## When To Use

Use this reference for conclusion sections, thesis conclusions, article closing sections, proposal endings, and final contribution statements.

## Core Function

A conclusion answers the "so what?" question. It should synthesize the document's argument, show how the research aim or question has been answered, state the contribution, acknowledge limits, and leave the reader with a clear sense of significance.

It should also evaluate the extent to which the study has addressed the original gap, aims, or questions. A strong conclusion is confident about what the evidence supports and careful about what remains unresolved.

## Recommended Structure

1. Return to the research problem or aim.
2. Synthesize the main findings or arguments.
3. State the contribution.
4. Explain significance for knowledge, method, practice, or future research.
5. Acknowledge limitations if not already handled fully.
6. Suggest future work or next steps.
7. End with a strong but proportionate final claim.

Short articles may combine several of these moves in one or two paragraphs. Long theses may need subsections.

## Thesis-Oriented Conclusion

Long conclusions often need to:

- remind readers of the research problem;
- answer each research question or objective;
- synthesize across chapters or studies;
- state original contribution;
- discuss implications;
- acknowledge limitations;
- propose future research;
- close with the broader value of the work.

Avoid a chapter-by-chapter summary that merely repeats what the reader has already read.

Some conclusions are more thesis-oriented: they return inward to the completed document and show how the project has answered its questions. Others are more field-oriented: they look outward to the discipline, profession, practice, or future research agenda. The best balance depends on genre and field. Some technical fields end strongly with future research; some humanities, social science, or practice-oriented fields give more space to implications and recommendations.

When planning a thesis conclusion, map it back to the introduction:

- gap or problem;
- research questions or aims;
- methods or evidence base;
- major findings;
- contribution and significance.

## Article-Oriented Conclusion

Article conclusions are usually shorter and more compressed:

- restate the main claim or finding;
- identify the contribution;
- acknowledge one or two key limits if appropriate;
- state the implication or next research direction.

Do not introduce a new literature review or new evidence in the conclusion.

## Contribution Claims

Contribution should be earned by the evidence and argument.

Types:

- empirical: new data, case, pattern, or finding;
- theoretical: concept, model, explanation, or refinement;
- methodological: design, measure, analytical approach, or dataset use;
- practical or policy-oriented: guidance for action, design, policy, or professional practice.

Useful language:

- This study contributes to...
- The analysis extends existing work by...
- The findings refine understanding of...
- The study provides evidence that...
- Methodologically, the research demonstrates...

Also consider whether the conclusion should address:

- applicability or generalizability;
- unexpected or contradictory outcomes;
- relevant comparison with prior research when needed to support the contribution;
- what the findings cannot show;
- how the contribution differs from existing work.

## Limitations And Future Work

Limitations and future work should be connected:

- Limitation: what this study could not do.
- Effect: how that shapes interpretation.
- Future work: what should be done next and why.

Weak pattern:

- "More research is needed" without specifying what kind of research, on what problem, and for what purpose.

Better pattern:

- Future work could examine X in Y contexts to determine whether Z applies beyond the present setting.

Separate practical recommendations from future-research recommendations:

- Practical recommendation: what a stakeholder, practitioner, designer, or decision-maker should consider doing.
- Future-research recommendation: what later studies should investigate and why.

Strong recommendations combine a concrete suggestion with justification from the findings.

Useful language:

- The findings suggest that practitioners should consider...
- A practical implication of this result is...
- Future research should examine...
- A priority for future work is...
- This limitation could be addressed by...

## Useful Closing Language

Purpose:

- This study set out to...
- This thesis examined...
- The research aimed to address...

Findings:

- Taken together, the findings show...
- Several conclusions can be drawn...
- The results obtained suggest...

Limitations:

- The findings should be interpreted in light of...
- The scope of the study was limited to...
- Caution is warranted when applying these findings to...

## Final Sentence Strategy

A strong final sentence is specific, proportionate, and connected to the study's central contribution.

Avoid:

- generic inspirational endings;
- inflated claims;
- abrupt endings after limitations;
- vague claims that the study is "important".

## Timing Guidance

- Plan the conclusion by tracking how each major finding answers a research question.
- Draft after the discussion and major analytical sections are stable.
- Revise near the end so aims, findings, discussion, abstract, and contribution claims align.

## Common Weak Patterns

- Repeating the introduction.
- Summarizing every section equally.
- Introducing new evidence or new theory.
- Overstating originality or impact.
- Ending with only limitations.
- Ending with only future research.
- Failing to answer the main research question.
- Mixing practical recommendations and future-research recommendations without distinction.
- Overstating applicability or generalizability.

## Checklist

- Does the conclusion return to the central problem?
- Does it synthesize rather than repeat?
- Are research questions or aims answered?
- Is the contribution clear and justified?
- Are implications specific?
- Are limitations proportionate?
- Is future work connected to the study's limits or findings?
- Does the ending leave the reader with a clear sense of significance?
