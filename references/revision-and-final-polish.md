# Revision and Final Polish

## When To Use

Use this reference for late-stage revision, section checking, proofreading strategy, consistency checks, and final academic-language polish.

## Core Principle

Revision is not only error correction. It moves from large-scale argument and structure to paragraph flow, sentence style, terminology, references, formatting, and final proofreading.

## Revision Order

1. Argument and purpose.
2. Section structure.
3. Paragraph function and flow.
4. Evidence and citation integration.
5. Claim strength and stance.
6. Academic register and concision.
7. Terminology consistency.
8. Reference and formatting checks.
9. Final proofreading.

Do not begin with comma-level editing if the section's purpose or structure is still weak.

## Whole-Section Diagnosis

Ask:

- What is this section supposed to achieve?
- Does the first paragraph prepare the reader?
- Are the major moves present?
- Is the order logical?
- Does each paragraph have a function?
- Are claims supported by evidence?
- Are key terms consistent?
- Does the section end by preparing the next section or closing the argument?

## Paragraph Revision

For each paragraph:

- identify the topic sentence;
- check whether the paragraph has one controlling idea;
- remove unrelated material;
- move background before complex claims;
- add interpretation after evidence;
- improve transitions by clarifying relationships, not merely adding connectors;
- ensure the final sentence is useful.

## Sentence-Level Polish

Prioritize:

- precise verbs;
- concrete nouns;
- controlled sentence length;
- accurate connectors;
- appropriate hedging;
- consistent tense;
- clear reference words;
- reduced wordiness;
- formal but readable phrasing.

Common edits:

- replace "there are many studies that show" with "Several studies show";
- replace "this is because of the fact that" with "because";
- replace "it is very important to note that" with the actual claim;
- replace informal phrasal verbs with precise academic verbs.

Common final-pass error types:

- subject-verb disagreement;
- malformed passive voice;
- comma splices;
- dangling modifiers;
- non-parallel structures;
- confusion between "imply" and "infer";
- confusion between "affect" and "effect";
- homophone errors such as "there", "their", and "they're";
- contractions and colloquial phrasing;
- cliches;
- run-on sentences;
- excessive personal pronouns where the genre expects a less personal style;
- informal sentence starters;
- rhetorical questions in formal prose;
- inconsistent citation style.

## Consistency Checks

Check consistency in:

- research questions and aims;
- key terms;
- variable or construct names;
- participant, sample, dataset, or case labels;
- page numbering and table of contents page numbers;
- table and figure numbering;
- table and figure titles, captions, and in-text callouts;
- spelling variants;
- citation style;
- tense and voice patterns;
- abbreviations.

## Reference And Evidence Checks

For each claim that depends on evidence:

- Is a citation or data reference provided?
- Is the citation relevant to the claim?
- Does the wording overstate what the evidence can support?
- Are quotations integrated and interpreted?
- Do direct quotations include page or location details when the citation style requires them?
- Are paraphrases sufficiently rephrased and cited?
- Are paraphrases and summaries cited even when no direct quotation is used?
- Are all in-text citations represented in the reference list, if a reference list is available?
- Are all reference-list items cited in the text, if a reference list is available?
- If similarity or originality checking is part of the workflow, are close paraphrases, uncited summaries, and overlong quotations reviewed carefully?

## Formatting And Submission Checks

For long documents or submission-ready manuscripts, check:

- pagination and page-number placement;
- table of contents entries and page numbers;
- figure and table numbering, titles, captions, and lists;
- abbreviations, symbols, appendices, and cross-references;
- appendix numbering and whether appendices are keyed in the text;
- heading levels and whether chapters or major sections must start on a new page;
- page breaks, missing text, stranded headings, and split tables;
- final PDF, print, or upload version quality.

Confirm current local submission procedures, forms, examination steps, or upload requirements with the authoritative target rules rather than relying on memory.

## Proofreading Strategy

- Rest the draft before proofreading when possible.
- Read slowly, one section at a time.
- Proofread one issue type at a time: sentence structure, word choice, spelling, punctuation, facts, figures, names, citations, and references.
- Check headings against content.
- Read paragraphs in reverse order to catch local problems.
- Read aloud or use text-to-speech to catch missing words and rhythm issues.
- Change the display format or print a copy if possible; a new visual format makes errors more visible.
- At the final stage, read word by word instead of reading only for meaning.
- Build a personal error list and search systematically for repeated problems.
- Search for repeated weak phrases.
- Check tables, figures, captions, and cross-references separately.
- Do a final pass for spelling, punctuation, and formatting.
- Leave time for late feedback, reference correction, formatting problems, and final file checks.

## Common Weak Patterns

- Polishing sentences before fixing structure.
- Making the prose smoother while leaving unsupported claims.
- Replacing precise technical terms with inaccurate synonyms.
- Removing hedging where caution is needed.
- Adding hedging to claims that are strongly supported.
- Accepting every suggested edit without checking meaning.
- Fixing only grammar while leaving paragraph logic unclear.

## Final Checklist

- Does the section perform its intended function?
- Are all major claims supported?
- Is the order reader-friendly?
- Are paragraphs cohesive?
- Is terminology consistent?
- Are connectors accurate and not overused?
- Is the academic register formal but readable?
- Are citations and references consistent?
- Are tables, figures, headings, and cross-references checked?
- Are pagination, table of contents entries, appendices, and final file quality checked?
- Have current submission or upload requirements been verified?
- Has a final proofreading pass been completed after major revisions?
